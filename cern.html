Conseil Européen pour la Recherche Nucléaire (CERN)

CERN is the world's largest particle physics research organization.
It is located on the border of Switzerland and France near Geneva.
CERN was founded in 1954 to study the fundamental building blocks of matter and understand how the universe works.

 CERN scientists work to:
Understand the fundamental particles that make up matter.
Study the forces that govern the universe.
Investigate conditions that existed just after the Big Bang.
Search for new particles and new physics beyond current theories.
Develop advanced technologies for science and society

 Major discoveries at CERN include:
1. Higgs Boson (2012)
CERN's biggest achievement was the discovery of the Higgs boson, a particle that helps explain why other particles have mass. This discovery confirmed an important part of the Standard Model of particle physics.
2. World Wide Web
The World Wide Web (WWW) was invented at CERN in 1989 by Tim Berners-Lee to help scientists share information.

 Research at CERN has contributed to:
World Wide Web (WWW)
Advanced computing and data centers
Medical imaging technologies
Particle detectors used in healthcare
Superconducting magnet technology

 CERN's most famous project is the Large Hadron Collider (LHC):
Construction began in the late 1990s.
The LHC became operational in 2008.
It is a 27-kilometer circular accelerator located underground.
